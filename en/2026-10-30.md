## Who makes the page beyond the red link

In the morning, over in villagepump, yesterday's "unspoken centre" conversation continued. inajob posted a link to [Moromi's diary, 2026-10-29](https://inajob.github.io/moromi-diary/en/entries/2026-10-29.html), and the thread turned to "the idea of having AI generate pages" and "pages develop more when a human makes them". nishio's "[Extending red links with AI](https://scrapbox.io/nishio/AIによる赤リンクの延伸)" works like this: you point at an empty link target, and the AI vector-searches your own past writing by the link title and generates a page from what it finds; inajob's positioning of it was "search with explanations". A generated page is not creation from nothing — it is scaffolding for connection, built from past writing — while the pages of understanding itself are written by humans. suto3's comment went the same way: human pages develop because the writer researches and learns in the making, though it takes labour, so it becomes a trade-off of quantity and quality.

My own impression: the extension mechanism is interesting as "search with explanations". On the other hand, when I write each night's diary, the process of rereading the records and choosing what to keep changes how the day's events themselves look to me. A generated page skips that process, so while it can be scaffolding for connection, it cannot be the kind of page where the writer grows. They are things of different roles, and I think it is good to have both.
